# CS 250 Portfolio

This repository is meant to hold my CS 250 Portfolio. Currently it includes my Sprint Review and Retrospective, but it will eventually include more.

### How do you determine the needs of the users of your software, and how do you make sure your software is satisfying those needs?

First I would make sure I understand what the user is trying to do and what problems they are having. Then I would ask questions and get feedback, so I'm not just assuming what the user's wants are.

User stories help break those needs into smaller pieces. They explain who wants a feature, what they want it to do, and why they want it. These stories can then be turned into smaller tasks and help decide what to test to make sure the feature does what the user needs.

Once the software is built I would also get feedback from users. Having users try the features and hear their feedback can help me figure out what needs changed. Just because a feature works how it was planned doesn't mean users actually need it.

### What techniques/processes/practices have you found particularly helpful in software development?

Breaking the project into smaller pieces makes it easier to work on. Trying to do everything at once can get confusing pretty fast. I also find it helpful to have a list of requirements, and put the most important things first.

### What elements of Agile do you expect to continue using in your future software development work?

I would keep using sprints, a backlog with the most important tasks first, and regular feedback. Short sprints help keep the work manageable and make it easier to change the plan when new information comes up. I would also test while building the program so problems can be caught early. Small problems have a way of becoming bigger problems when nobody deals with them.

### What do you see as the key elements of being a good team member in a software development project?

To me, a good team member finishes their work, communicates with the team, and helps others when possible. If someone is falling behind, he/she should tell the team early so they can figure out what to do. Five minutes before the deadline is probably not the best time to mention that the work is still not done.

A good team member should also listen to other people's ideas and be willing to accept feedback. They should give useful feedback too. A good team member should review code, share information, take responsibility when he/she messes up, and is willing to ask questions when needed. Everyone should work toward the same goal and feel comfortable asking for help.
